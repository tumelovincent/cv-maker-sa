Job interview tips

An invitation to an interview means your CV worked. Now the employer wants to see whether you are the right person. A little preparation makes a big difference to how calm and clear you are on the day.

Updated 24 October 2026 · By Tumelo Vincent Tau

1. Before the day

  • Read the advert again and your own CV: they will ask about both.
  • Learn about the employer: what they do, where they are and what the job involves.
  • Plan the trip: the address, the transport, how long it takes and money for the taxi. Aim to arrive 15 minutes early.
  • Practise out loud with a friend or in front of a mirror, using the questions below.

2. What to bring

  • Your ID
  • A printed copy of your CV, and of your cover letter if you sent one
  • Your certificates (matric, qualifications) and copies, especially if the invitation asks for them
  • A pen, and the name and number of the person who invited you

3. Questions you will probably be asked

QuestionHow to answer
Tell us about yourself.One minute: your education, your experience and why you want this job. Not your life story.
Why do you want to work here?Something real about the employer or the work, and how it fits what you want to learn.
What are your strengths?Two strengths that matter for this job, each with a short example.
What is your weakness?A real one that is not central to the job, and what you are doing to improve it.
Tell us about a time when…Use the STAR method below.
Do you have any questions for us?Always ask one or two, for example what a normal day looks like or when they will decide.

4. The STAR method for "tell us about a time" questions

Many employers, and government panels in particular, ask for examples of what you did. Answer in four short steps:

  • S - Situation: where you were and what was happening
  • T - Task: what you had to do
  • A - Action: what you did, in your own words ("I", not "we")
  • R - Result: how it ended, and what you learnt
Example: "At the tuck shop a customer was angry because we had run out of bread (S). I had to keep him as a customer (T). I apologised, phoned the bakery and kept two loaves aside for him the next morning (A). He came back and still buys from us (R)."

5. On the day

  • Dress neatly and a little more formally than the job, with clean, ironed clothes and shoes.
  • Greet everyone politely, including the security guard and receptionist.
  • Switch your phone to silent before you go in.
  • Listen to the whole question, and ask them to repeat it if you did not understand. Take a breath before you answer.
  • Be honest. If you do not know something, say so and say how you would find out.
  • Some employers add a test or practical task. If the invitation mentions one, ask what it involves.

6. Afterwards

  • Send a short thank-you message or email the same day, naming the post.
  • If you have not heard anything by the date they gave, a polite follow-up call is fine.
  • Write down the questions they asked: they help you prepare for the next interview.
