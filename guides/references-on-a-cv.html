References on your CV

References are people an employer can phone to ask about you. South African employers often call them before they offer a job, so good references can make the difference.

Updated 24 October 2026 · By Tumelo Vincent Tau

1. How many and where

  • List two or three references in a section at the end of your CV.
  • Most South African employers expect to see them on the CV. If you write "available on request", have the details ready to send the same day.

2. Who to choose

Your situationGood references
You have worked beforeA manager or supervisor from your last job or two, or a senior colleague who saw your work.
First job or school leaverA teacher, principal or lecturer; a sports coach; someone you did piece work for; a supervisor from volunteering or a learnership.
Community and characterA pastor, community leader or a business owner who knows you well. Use one of these, not all three.

Avoid family members and close friends: employers want someone who can speak about your work and behaviour honestly.

3. What to write for each person

Mr Sipho Dlamini
Store Manager, ABC Supermarket, Welkom
My manager from 2024 to 2025
072 000 0000 · sipho@example.co.za
  • Full name and title (Mr, Ms, Dr)
  • Their job title and where they work
  • How they know you, and for how long
  • A phone number that works, and an email address if they have one

4. Always ask first

Ask each person before you put them on your CV. It is their personal information, and a reference who expects the call gives a much better answer.

  • Tell them which jobs you are applying for, so they can mention the right strengths.
  • Check the number before every round of applications: a reference who never answers is worse than none.
  • Thank them, and let them know when you get the job.

5. Common mistakes

  • Old numbers that no longer work
  • References who do not remember you, or did not know they were listed
  • A previous employer you left on bad terms, with no other work reference
  • Only family members or friends
